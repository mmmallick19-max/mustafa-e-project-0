Scientific Research

Exploring the discoveries that transformed modern science.

Understanding Radioactivity

Marie Curie carried out pioneering research into radiation emitted by uranium and other substances. She introduced the term radioactivity to describe this phenomenon.

Working with Pierre Curie, she investigated pitchblende, a uranium-rich mineral. Their measurements showed that the mineral was more radioactive than uranium alone could explain.

This led them to investigate unknown radioactive substances and eventually announce the discovery of polonium and radium in 1898.

How the Research Worked

1. Mineral samples: The Curies studied pitchblende and other uranium-containing materials.

2. Measurements: They used sensitive instruments, including an electrometer developed with Jacques Curie's help, to measure electrical effects caused by radiation.

3. Chemical separation: They processed the mineral and separated its chemical components.

4. New elements: Their investigations led to the identification of polonium and radium.

5. Scientific impact: Their findings helped establish radioactivity as an important field of scientific research.

Why Was This Research Important?

Marie Curie's research changed scientific understanding of atoms and radioactive materials. It also contributed to later developments in medical diagnosis and radiation treatment. However, the dangers of radiation exposure were not fully understood during her lifetime.
