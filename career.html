Scientific Career

A lifetime of research, discovery, and dedication.

Marie Curie's Professional Life

Marie Curie's career was built around scientific investigation. Her work on radioactivity helped transform physics and chemistry.

1897–1898

Research on Uranium

Marie investigated uranium rays and found that the radiation came from the atoms themselves. She introduced the term radioactivity.

1898

Discovery of Polonium

Marie and Pierre Curie announced the discovery of polonium, named in honor of Marie's homeland, Poland.

1898

Discovery of Radium

The Curies announced the discovery of radium, a highly radioactive element, through their work with mineral samples.

1906

Professor at Sorbonne

Following Pierre Curie's death, Marie took over his teaching position and became the first woman to teach as a professor at the Sorbonne.

1914–1918

Mobile X-Ray Units

During World War I, she helped develop and operate mobile X-ray units to assist doctors in treating wounded soldiers.

1920s

Scientific Leadership

She continued directing research and supporting the development of scientific institutions and laboratories.
